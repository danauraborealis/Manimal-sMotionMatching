# Moving hit-reaction prototype

Opt-in and selected-test-bot only. `SetHitReactions(enabled, synthetic)` arms it; normal fleet bots are unaffected. Local Valve-derived `reaction_posedb.json` belongs beside the plugin DLL and is not distributed in the install archive.

## Trigger policy

Every positive post-armor health-damage event rolls against `min(85%, 10% + 0.5% × damage in the last 2 seconds)`. An isolated 45 damage hit has a 32.5% chance; 50 damage has a 35% chance. There is no damage threshold. Old damage expires from a sliding window. A selected animation actually starting begins a 2.5 second cooldown; rejected entries do not consume it. Cooldown hits do not queue another reaction or build probability.

The synthetic mode sends damage events without modifying health and uses the same probability policy. It is for recorded automation; `-ManualGallery` disables it, releases camera tracking and prepares 20 back-and-forth passes in the saved test area. Manual targets receive 10,000 HP per body part through native live health values. The health change is confined to that bot in the current raid; no profile/database health settings are changed.

## Playback and limitations

Successful hit rolls choose between upper recoil and a direction/speed-compatible stumble. Stumble preference rises continuously from 15% to 65% as damage probability rises; upper-only selection independently weights light versus heavy clips by that normalized pressure. If no stumble enters within its admission window, a compatible upper hit is tried. Standing targets can receive upper hits. Moving, grounded, non-sprinting entries can select a stumble. Standing, jumping, prone, rapid turns and incompatible foot support retain native animation. A 0.2 second entry window waits for a suitable step. Planted feet remain within 20 cm; a swinging foot can be rebased through the placer's existing entry path, bounded to 55 cm horizontal and 85 cm total source difference. The latter is not a guarantee of visual quality.

The local database now includes direction-retargeted chest, shoulder and arm rotations (`upperBodyRotations`). A shared post-VisualPass layer applies them after Tarkov hand IK, preserving native bone translations, wrist rotations and finger pose. The active firearm follows its right-palm grip; grenade hands and firearm hide/draw operations release the layer without moving a holstered gun. The support hand is constrained back to its per-frame native weapon-relative target after upper motion. A two-bone rotational solve preserves arm lengths and wrist orientation. If right-arm motion moves the weapon beyond support-arm reach, arm/collar excursion is attenuated toward native while torso motion remains. This also preserves native reload hand tasks instead of caching a permanent grip. This is an explicit change from the earlier arms-pinned prototype, requested by the user. The expanded local database preserves the four directional stumbles and adds five upper hits: light 02/35 and heavier 06/20/11. These five use source rotation changes relative to frame zero over native upper motion; they never replace legs or drive the root. Torso/arm amplitudes are 40%/25% for light hits and 65%/50% for heavier hits. Source pelvis/chest change is rebased into the current pelvis, preserving spine translations. These masks are initial tuning values, not final visual acceptance.

Upper-body entry uses 0.2 seconds of velocity-aware inertia; exit uses a bounded 0.5-second smoothstep decay from the displayed pose, without angular-velocity extrapolation. Interrupted recovery remains continuous at re-entry. The source clip plays to its terminal frame unless movement ownership changes. The old final-0.14-second pose fade and arbitrary 0.85-second duration limit are removed. Old databases without upper tracks retain the rotation-only torso fallback.

During a stumble, sampled horizontal root velocity drives the existing collision-aware movement path with bounded acceleration. Native stops, jumps and expired commands release that drive. Vertical motion, firing decisions, ragdolls and damage calculation remain native. Visible weapon orientation follows the reacting arm and therefore may diverge from the AI's aiming intention during the brief reaction. Spine translation is never pinned to its previous world position.

The correct grunt graph uses separate `wl_stumble_shorthops` and `wl_hit_reactions_bonemask` routes. The stumble graph has 0.1-second entry and condition-dependent exits of 0.5, 1 or 2 seconds; stumble root-motion blend is 1, ordinary masked hit-react root-motion blend is 0. The local upper-body layer is a Tarkov adaptation, not a claim of exact Valve mask parity: unlisted-bone inheritance and mask-child polarity were not fully established by the graph audit. See `tmp/alyx/grunt_active_graph.vanmgrph` and `tmp/alyx/grunt_sequences.txt`; the earlier locomotion graph findings document is not authoritative for this active reaction graph.

Forward starts within 45 degrees of facing now use native Tarkov acceleration/animation, including restarting from Hold. The experimental authored start upper-body layer was removed after visual feedback. Authored side/back starts and stops remain. Editing a forward source clip is deferred; any future experiment should be limited to one clip matched against native foot phase, root speed and torso pose.

Deferred native-loop admission continues retained authored starts to their end while retrying support-compatible entry. Sole support replaces raw ankle contact for the flight handoff shortcut. Idle waits for both lower and upper recovery before admitting stops or reacquiring a cycle. Reaction handoff consumes the standing-start intent so recovery cannot spuriously trigger a new Start. Grossly mismatched immediate stop poses yield to native braking with a retry cooldown. Start/Stop/Hold retain native pelvis tilt and compensate thigh rotations.

## Verification history

Earlier builds rejected every stumble. Sole-support matching, a wider entry-frame search and separate swing-foot admission produced full `stumble_n` playback in live gallery captures. The 20260918-002025 run played two full reactions; 20260918-002739 played three. These are not visual acceptance results: the latter showed entry jumps and a roughly 40 cm foot jump near exit. Subsequent changes preserve velocity at entry and release remaining placement correction before the low-weight cutoff, rather than resetting it. Run 20260918-003657 played three stumbles from four requests. The roughly 40 cm placement-added exit jump was absent during reaction playback, but some source/pose foot steps still reached 8–9 cm per frame. Root-motion driving and removal of torso position pinning were added after that capture; their live verification is separate.

Pure tests cover damage-weighted probability, expiration, rejected requests, actual-play cooldown, invalid values, support preservation and bounded swing entry. Runtime metadata/package checks enforce a DLL-only archive and the installed UnityToolkit dependency.

Run 20260918-004537 verified four completed stumbles with horizontal root driving and no spine position pinning. Across 3,706 final bone samples, pelvis-to-Spine1 length varied by 0.563 mm, versus 99.655 mm in the preceding build. This validates removal of the stretch; full movement aesthetics and the start/loop handoff still need work.

Run 20260918-013012 completed four reactions from four triggers with native forward starts. Recovery measurements and phase counts are recorded in artifacts/reaction-exit-bounded.json. This is a skeleton/capture regression check, not final mesh animation acceptance.


## Landing prototype and future tuning

With the reaction prototype armed, an observed moving Jump episode may trigger one reduced stumble-derived upper overlay after all native jump/landing ownership releases. It requires ground, a firearm carry context, current movement, a direction-compatible source, at least 0.1 seconds of observed jump ownership, and takeoff speed at least 0.8 m/s. Admission expires after 0.3 seconds. Vault/climb states cancel the candidate; JumpLanding alone is insufficient. The overlay uses 30% torso and 20% arm motion; native legs/root remain in control. It does not consume the hit-damage cooldown or increment hit counts. No animation runs in the airborne phase.

Overlays are serialized in capture Pose.UpperOverlay/UpperOverlayFrame. tools/analyze_upper_overlays.py reports episodes, overlap with native jump ownership, and recovery rotation steps; tools/render_upper_body_replay.py accepts --phase hit or landing. The expanded database is built by tools/build_reaction_database.py under tmp/alyx and stays out of runtime archives.

Weight/boss modifiers are **not implemented**. See weight_reaction_plan.md for the requested deferred design and its unapproved example numbers. Current damage roll remains unchanged.

The reactionshowcase scenario explicitly previews all five upper hits, followed by n/s/e/w stumbles during matching directional movement. This diagnostic bypasses damage probability without changing the normal hit policy; named stumbles still require compatible entry. Use Run-PuppetTest.ps1 -OpenArea to find and retain a flat open center for this test, rather than the usual preferred lane.
